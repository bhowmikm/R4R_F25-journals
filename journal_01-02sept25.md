## Research Application

Even though I am not conducting research yet, the week’s material is still very relevant to my academic work. For example, in my lab courses I regularly generate data tables that I later have to use to answer questions or build graphs. Applying Open Science practices could make that process smoother. Using “Accessible” principles could mean saving my files in widely used formats (like CSV instead of only Excel) so I could open them on different computers. “Interoperable” could mean keeping my calculations and graphs in a way that can be easily re-created in another program, rather than only in the lab manual.
Another application is in my written reports. When I turn in a report for chemistry or physiology, my professor often asks for clear organization and reproducibility, in other words, someone else should be able to understand and repeat what I did. That’s exactly what Open Science emphasizes. If I learn to apply this mindset now, even in small course assignments, I will be more prepared when I eventually transition into independent research.
